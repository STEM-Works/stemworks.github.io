Building the Next Generation of Engineers

Our programs are designed to introduce students to real-world engineering in a way that is engaging, hands-on, and fun. Through guided instruction and open-ended challenges, students learn how engineers think, design, and solve problems. By the end of the program, participants gain confidence in their technical abilities while developing creativity, teamwork, and problem-solving skills that extend far beyond the classroom.

About STEM Works

At STEM Works, we believe that the best way to learn engineering is by building real systems from start to finish. Our workshops are developed and led by engineering students who understand both the technical depth and the challenges of learning complex concepts for the first time. We focus on creating an inclusive environment where students are encouraged to experiment, ask questions, and learn from failure as part of the engineering process.

During Weeks 1-2, students are introduced to our Game Manual, which explains what their robots should be able to accomplish. We provide a demonstration on how to solder wires, followed by a hands-on session where students solder their own connections.

Students also get an introduction to the electrical components used in our program, including ESP32 microcontrollers, LN motor drivers, and servo motors. Additionally, we cover CAD fundamentals using OnShape and guide students in creating 3D models of their robots.

×

During Weeks 3-4, students receive the 3D printed models of the robots they designed in the previous week. They are given time to assemble and manufacture their robots and test whether all the components work properly.

This hands-on phase helps students understand how their CAD designs translate into real robots, and allows them to troubleshoot any assembly issues before moving on to programming and fine tuning.

×

Week 5 is focused on programming the robots. Students start with a test code and must iterate it to match their individual wiring and robot configurations, since each robot is customized.

During this week, we also hold driver tryouts, allowing students to compete in a fun and engaging way while determining who is best suited to drive their robot during the final competition.

×

The final week brings everything together in an exciting competition environment. Teams put their robots to the test, applying everything they've learned throughout the program. Students reflect on their design decisions, teamwork, and problem-solving approaches while celebrating their accomplishments in a supportive and rewarding setting.

×

Students gain exposure to industry-relevant tools and technologies used by professional engineers. By working with microcontrollers, motors, CAD software, and control systems, participants develop practical skills that prepare them for future academic and career opportunities in STEM fields.
